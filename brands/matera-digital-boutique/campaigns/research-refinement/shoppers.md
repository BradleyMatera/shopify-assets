# Matera Digital Boutique: Buyer intent for the first ten guide promotions

## What the evidence supports

**Start with “Measure first / choose on purpose,” not generic lifestyle copy.** People typing into Google described tangible constraints: deep shelves, narrow hallways, small kitchens and desks, bathroom tile, refrigerator or freezer use. That is a plausible creative angle, not a validated revenue forecast. Put pantry, drawers, and food storage first: the [2026 U.S. Houzz Kitchen Trends Study](https://www.houzz.com/press/1006/Homeowners-Turn-to-Specialty-Built-In-Features-in-Kitchen-Renovations-Houzz-Study-Finds) reported pantry cabinets (47%) and deep drawer organizers (34%) among the home-renovation features it examined. Its 1,780 Houzz-user renovation sample was surveyed in July 2025, so it is directional context for kitchen function, not general-market retail demand or a late-November sales count. The [2026 Houzz & Home research recap](https://www.houzz.com/magazine/11-things-to-know-about-home-renovations-today-stsetivw-vs~185079173) also lists organizational products among reported purchases; still not Boutique conversion evidence.

On the research date, public US-English Google autocomplete responded to all ten seeds below. I sampled each once in a non-signed-in HTTP client and quoted only useful examples. [Google's own methodology](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533) explains that autocomplete is influenced by location and filtering and is not a popularity leaderboard. The public [Google Trends U.S. 12-month comparison](https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%2012-m&geo=US&q=pantry%20organization,kitchen%20drawer%20organization,entryway%20organization) could not be retrieved with usable comparable chart values. No volume, seasonal peak, keyword difficulty, ranking, or comparative search traffic is claimed.

## What would actually validate it

- Use 10 distinct editorial ads across the rollout, one decision per creative. For Facebook, a question and direct tracked guide; for Pinterest, search-shaped title and native proof/guide graphic; for Instagram, clearly direct to the verified clickable Story/profile path where available. Use the exact article, not the uncontrolled wider collection. These articles lack explicit sharing images; attach the appropriate art.
- Use the existing creative IDs and tracked URLs rather than inventing a second tracking taxonomy mid-sprint. Compare tagged human sessions per verified live post and, where available, outbound click rate and saves separately; monitor reach rather than treating no clicks on no reach as a rejection. Track product explorations, carts, and real orders separately. Don't assume native analytics ingested a test because the loader exists; reconcile a distinct QA-click tag and exclude it.
- To validate quantitatively later, export owner-authorized Search Console queries and page-level clicks/impressions on the new domain where available, and use a common bridge term such as pantry organization when comparing small Google Trends sets on the same U.S. period. Preserve filters. Trends 0–100 is relative to the comparison, not search counts; no assertion of demand or competitiveness from autocomplete order or 0 values. This is later research, not a blocker for truthful guide promotion.
- Product promotion remains separate. No inventory, discounts, price, fulfillment, specs, health, certifications, or compatibility claim without exact-product evidence.
