# Boutique demand creative: release-quality audit

**Scope:** 200 editorial candidates and the ten distinct Facebook guide release records. These are creative judgments, not engagement or revenue results.

## What actually repeats

- The 200 CSV rows are **50 topics plus four platform adaptations per topic** across ten families, not 200 independent ads. There are 50 distinct shot briefs, reused across all four platforms. Exact string uniqueness does not demonstrate different motion, pacing, proof, or visual language.
- **200/200** route to only two landing paths: 140 to the generic buying-guide library and 60 to the homepage. Unique UTM strings do not create distinct landing experiences. A shopper shown a specific problem should normally land on the exact matching guide or a vetted item, not hunt through a hub.
- **Facebook:** 50/50 CTAs use one of two patterns, “Browse the…” (35) or “Explore the Boutique…” (15). Better hooks include “A drawer can be wider than its opening,” which sets up a clear reveal. “What is the least glamorous spot you use most?” lacks a concrete object, consequence, or proof until the body. Optimize the first sentence for a real buyer, not the internal concept label.
- **Instagram:** 50/50 captions send people “via the link,” yet a single bio URL cannot magically resolve to fifty different per-post URLs. Use an eligible Story link to the exact destination, or deliberately send to a verified bio hub and label attribution as placement-level. Feed creative must still deliver useful proof standalone.
- **Pinterest:** 50/50 candidate CTA fields begin with “Save the”; the first families repeat endings such as “Try this when resetting your workspace” and “Keep this measurement cue handy.” Convert titles to descriptive search intent: object + specific fit problem, not archive labels. Let the graphic earn the save and the destination answer the next question.
- **YouTube:** 50/50 hooks begin “Try this” and default to opening card / visual action / voiceover / end card. Use actual footage, first-frame conflict, on-screen proof, and an editorial payoff. These are conditional concepts, not uploaded clips. A Short’s copy must not promise a tappable description link.
- **Facebook release audit:** The dated release log records 10 guide releases (and one separate Black Friday editorial, excluded here), covering drawers, food containers, pantry, appliances, bath mats, adhesive hooks, entryways, lighting, closet racks, and desks. The ten landing guides are much better matched than the 200-candidate hub paths. The repository release register contains content keys, dates, state, and spend, not complete captions. Three representative native captions were reviewed via captured excerpts: the drawer starts with the daily utensil hidden behind six unused items; the food-storage ad starts with mismatched lids despite tidy photos; workstation starts with a desk fitting the wall while wedging the chair. Those are identifiable shopper problems, not generic hooks. All three continue with an instructional list and a guide CTA: maintain useful specificity, but vary the payoff and actually show the annoyance instead of explaining it every time. No wording claims are made for the other seven live captions. Across those ten recorded angles, 8/10 are explicitly check/compare/measure before shopping; repetition here is an angle-level finding, **not** a count of repeated live caption strings. The ten are educational traffic acquisition, not direct product or conversion proof. The separate Black Friday line, “Measure the space, not the deal,” is a sharper editorial contrast, but still works only if its imagery demonstrates the check rather than implying a discount. Avoid adding another identical batch to the same Page: alternate demonstrations, surprises, questions, and actual editorial story arcs.

## Approval rubric (0–3 points each)

| Dimension | 0–1: rewrite | 2: adequate | 3: release standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shopping tension | Generic idea, slogan or fuzzy question | Names a real object and problem | Names what fails or costs the shopper, immediately |
| Opening/recognition | Logo-first; invisible in the first frame | Understandable within 2 seconds | Specific first frame makes the problem recognizable without sound |
| Observable proof | Claim or stock photo only | Real or labeled illustrative example | A safe, reproducible test, comparison, or before/after shows the difference |
| Creative distinction | Same visual/CTA script with nouns swapped | Different visual beat or format | Distinct mechanism, shot, and payoff from adjacent creative |
| Offer/landing match | Generic hub, wrong identity, or ambiguous availability | Relevant verified page and action | Exact decision-specific destination; no needless steps |
| Platform execution | Misleading link or mobile unreadable | Correct crop and eligible placement | Platform-native link, short copy, readable captions/alt and sound-off proof |

**Release only at 15/18 or better**, with at least 2 on every dimension. Automatic hold overrides score for wrong publisher, unverifiable link, unsourced factual claim, unapproved spend, unreadable text, private/personal data, variable inventory, or product details that cannot be independently rechecked. A companion Story used as a link affordance is not a second published concept. Example copy-only review: the workstation hook earns 3/3 for tension (desk fits, chair does not); the matching guide is a strong 3/3 landing match if final click verification passes. Leave first-frame and observable-proof scores unset until the actual visual is inspected; do not award a passing total from copy alone. A Facebook row ending in a generic hub fails the landing gate even with a 3/3 hook.

## 12 shopper-led ad scripts

Guide links below are relative to `https://materadigitalboutique.myshopify.com`. Recheck exact published destination, current title, and attribution at release. The two direct commerce scripts are conditional: verify actual product, selection, stock, market shipping, margin and checkout before publication. Use actual approved photographs for seller-known products and label scenes with unrelated props as illustrative if they could be mistaken for items included. No fake deals or reviews.
